How to hide a window from screen sharing and recording

Updated 6 November 2026

When you share your screen on Zoom, Teams, Google Meet, or record it with OBS, those apps ask the operating system for a copy of what is on screen. On Windows, a window can tell the operating system "leave me out of that copy." When it does, the window stays on your display but never appears in the shared or recorded video. This is a real, built-in feature - not a trick.

The Windows mechanism

Windows exposes a per-window display-affinity setting. An app can mark its own window as excluded from capture, and from then on the compositor paints that window to your monitor but omits it from any screen capture - whether that capture comes from a meeting app, a recorder, or a screenshot tool. You keep seeing the window; the capture does not. This is why a correctly built overlay can be visible to you and invisible on a shared screen at the same time.

Why macOS is different

macOS used to allow something similar, but since macOS 15 Apple routes screen capture through a framework that does not let an app hide its own windows from capture. On a current Mac, any window you can see can also be captured. So an overlay that is invisible on Windows will be visible in a screen share on macOS. Any tool that claims full invisibility on a modern Mac is overpromising.
